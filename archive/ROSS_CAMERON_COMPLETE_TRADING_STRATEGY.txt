1. STOCK SCANNER / STOCK SELECTION CRITERIA
================================================================================

THE FIVE CORE PILLARS OF STOCK SELECTION (MANDATORY CRITERIA):
These are non-negotiable. A stock must meet ALL FIVE or it should not be traded.

PILLAR ONE - PRICE RANGE:
- Preferred range: $2 to $20 per share (the "sweet spot")
- Under $2: Problematic due to massive sell orders and liquidity issues
- Over $20: Less interesting for small account traders seeking 10%+ moves
- Note: Generally avoid stocks under $1 (too risky)
- Real example: Ross preferred not to trade below his price cutoff

PILLAR TWO - PERCENTAGE CHANGE (RATE OF CHANGE):
- CRITICAL RULE: Stock must be up AT LEAST 10% MINIMUM from previous close
- Not only up 10%, but must have potential to go ANOTHER 10%
- 99% of Ross's profit comes from stocks up 10% minimum
- If a stock doesn't have 10% upside potential from your entry point, DO NOT TRADE IT
- Example: Bank of America at $39/share would need to move to $42.90+ - unlikely
- Good candidates: Stocks up 50¢-$1+ in initial moves with momentum (e.g., CNUI from 65¢ to $1+)

PILLAR THREE - RELATIVE VOLUME (RV):
- Minimum RV ratio: 5 (minimum threshold)
- Stocks should trade 5+ times their average daily volume
- Example: WKY had relative volume of 96 (trading 96 times higher than average)
- This shows abnormal buying pressure and strong demand
- RV is critical because it's context-dependent - 1M shares might or might not be "high"

PILLAR FOUR - NEWS/CATALYST:
- Stock must have news or catalyst triggering the move
- Types of catalysts: Company announcements, earnings surprises, FDA approval, partnerships, etc.
- Example: Breaking news about SpaceX satellite launch caused immediate massive move
- News creates the imbalance between supply and demand that drives big moves
- Time frame: Track when news was released (within 2 hours = red flame, 2-12 hours = orange)

PILLAR FIVE - FLOAT:
- Preferred float: UNDER 20 MILLION SHARES
- Lower is better (1M, 2M, 10M, 15M all good)
- 18-19 million shares: Getting high
- Above 20 million: Generally not as interesting (fewer shares = easier to push higher)
- Exception noted: Gamestop in 2021 had 36M share float - but was exception
- Typical trade example: ONCO around 23M share float (at the border but acceptable)

BONUS CRITERIA (Not mandatory but improve setup quality):
- News recency and type
- Stock's recent history and trend
- Market-wide conditions
- Catalyst strength

2. ENTRY PATTERNS & SETUPS
================================================================================

PRIMARY SETUP: "FIRST CANDLE MAKES NEW HIGH"

EXACT ENTRY RULE:
- Entry triggers when the FIRST CANDLE makes a new high versus the high of the previous candle
- This is a breakout setup - price breaks above recent resistance
- Requires confirmation from multiple indicators (MACD and volume must align)

SETUP REQUIREMENTS (ALL MUST BE MET):
1. MACD must be "open" (positive, showing upward momentum)
2. Volume profile must be positive (buying volume > selling volume)
3. No high volume red candles (no rejection of price)
4. Light volume selling acceptable, high volume selling is a rejection/red flag
5. Stock must be above the moving averages (9, 20, and 200 EMA) on interday chart

SETUP VARIATIONS:

SQUEEZED CONSOLIDATION SETUP:
- Stock consolidates (sideways movement) for several candles
- MACD begins to "squeeze" - the two lines converge
- Watch for expansion phase when MACD lines diverge again (opening)
- First break from squeeze with volume = entry signal
- BEST TRADES occur on front side of move before moving averages converge
- Once moving averages converge = choppy sideways consolidation phase (avoid trading)

MOMENTUM CONTINUATION SETUP:
- Stock shows strong directional move with opening MACD
- Dips slightly within the trend
- Dip becomes entry point when it respects moving average support
- Volume picks up on the bounce

NEWS-DRIVEN BREAKOUT SETUP:
- Breaking news catalyst released
- Stock gaps up significantly at open or during day
- Initial breakout phase with heavy volume
- Look for first leg of move before consolidation
- Stocks on very beginning of breaking news squeeze are best (red/orange flame)

PROBABILITY ASSESSMENT FOR SETUPS:
- Perfect setup should have 80% probability of working
- A setup that looks like it might work: Consider smaller position size (not mandatory trade)
- "Hard yes" = clear signals - full size position
- Uncertain signals = reduce position size or skip
- Rule: If it's not a "hard yes," it's a NO
- Beginner strategy: If uncertain, pass (protect capital)

WHAT A HARD "YES" LOOKS LIKE:
- MACD is clearly open (well separated lines)
- Volume profile shows strong buying (green bars, light red)
- Stock positioned above moving averages
- First candle breaks recent resistance
- No warning signals from any indicator

WHAT A "NO" LOOKS LIKE (DO NOT TAKE):
- High volume red candles (heavy selling)
- MACD is closed, flat, or crossed over (negative)
- Stock below moving averages
- "Topping tails" on candles (rejection wicks)
- Choppy action with indecision

3. ENTRY TIMING & SIGNALS
================================================================================

EXACT ENTRY TRIGGER:
When the first new candle makes a new high compared to the previous candle's high

OPTIMAL ENTRY CONDITIONS:

TIMING IN TRADING DAY:
- Early morning opportunities important (4 AM - 10 AM market hours matter)
- Pre-9:30 AM: Many stocks moving early (7 AM, 8 AM, 9 AM action)
- 9:30 AM - 10 AM: Peak action and volatility often occurs at market open
- First leg of moves typically better than later consolidations
- Trading is best when MACD is open (not converged/squeezed)

VOLUME CONFIRMATION REQUIREMENTS:

BUYING VOLUME SIGNALS:
- Volume bars on entry candle should be green or show buying pressure
- Light volume selling acceptable (shows buying dominates)
- Heavy buying volume on entry confirms setup

SELLING VOLUME RED FLAGS:
- High volume red candles = major rejection signal
- High volume selling on price attempts = STOP (do not buy)
- Rule: If just ONE indicator says "NO," the answer is NO

CANDLESTICK SIGNALS FOR ENTRY:

GOOD ENTRY CANDLE CHARACTERISTICS:
- Large green body (strong buying)
- Small or no upper wick (not rejected)
- Closes near high (buyers in control)
- Volume higher than average (confirms demand)

POOR ENTRY CANDLE CHARACTERISTICS:
- "Topping tails" (long upper wicks showing rejection)
- Red candles with high volume
- Closes near lows (sellers in control)
- Spinning tops or dojis (indecision)

INDICATOR ENTRY SIGNALS REQUIRED:

MACD SIGNALS:
- Must be "open" for entry (9 EMA separated above 20 EMA by good margin)
- MACD histogram shows positive momentum
- DO NOT BUY when MACD is flat or crossed over (negative)
- MACD cross-over = signal to exit existing trades

MOVING AVERAGE ALIGNMENT:
- Primarily trade when stock is ABOVE the moving averages
- Valid on 1-minute, 5-minute, and daily charts
- Price above 9 EMA, 9 EMA above 20 EMA, 20 EMA above 200 EMA = uptrend
- When price falls below moving averages = downtrend (avoid buying)

4. STOP LOSS RULES
================================================================================

MAXIMUM DAILY LOSS LIMITS:

GENERAL RULE:
- Keep share size well within "loss comfort levels" to manage emotions
- If you lose more than your loss comfort zone allows, it affects psychology and future trades

POSITION-SPECIFIC STOP LOSS:

PRIMARY STOP LOSS METHOD:
- Set stop loss below the low of the consolidation candle or previous swing low
- Exact placement depends on setup:
  - Tight consolidations = stop just below last candle low
  - Wide consolidations = stop below lowest point in consolidation zone
  - First candle break = stop below the candle before the break

MAXIMUM RISK PER TRADE:
- Keep position size small enough that a stop loss hit is manageable emotionally
- A single stop loss hit should not damage your confidence or account significantly
- Example: If your loss comfort is $200-300, don't take $500 risk trades

PSYCHOLOGICAL STOP LOSS RULES:
- If a trade is against you and doesn't immediately move in your favor = exit
- Don't hold losers hoping for reversal - "cut your losses quickly"
- Being a good trader = "being good at losing" (cutting losses fast)
- Once you enter, if it moves wrong = get out immediately
- No "averaging down" into losing positions

DAILY LOSS LIMITS:
- Have a predetermined daily loss limit
- Once hit, stop trading for the day
- Ross's account structure: Keeps only $150,000 in trading account max
- This limits total possible daily loss in absolute dollar terms

STOP LOSS EXIT SIGNALS:
1. First candle after entry goes lower - reversal signal
2. Price falls below moving average support after entry
3. MACD crosses over to negative after entry
4. High volume selling candles after your entry
5. Two consecutive lower closes after entry

EXIT IMMEDIATELY IF:
- Stock gaps down significantly at open after you're in overnight
- Breaking news comes out that contradicts the original catalyst
- Volume dries up completely (loss of interest)

5. PROFIT TAKING / EXIT STRATEGY
================================================================================

EXIT TIMING RULES:

BASIC EXIT PRINCIPLE:
- When you get into a trade, price should move up immediately
- If it does: "Perfect timing" - ride the momentum
- If it doesn't move up right away: Be cautious

TRAILING EXIT PATTERN:
- As stock moves up, look for the first candle that makes a new lower close than previous
- When you see two consecutive candles going lower: Consider exiting
- Don't let winners turn into losers

SCALING OUT STRATEGY:
- Partial exits on specific price targets
- Add to winners rather than averaging into losers
- Take smaller profits early, let bigger portions run
- Reduces risk by locking in gains on portion of position

PROFIT TARGET RULES:

CONSERVATIVE TARGETS:
- 10% move minimum from entry (targets $0.20-$1.00+ depending on entry price)
- First target: Take 10-20% of position off at 10% gain

RUNNER PORTION:
- Keep remaining 80% position as "runner"
- Let winners run until trailing stop hits
- Some trades move 50-100%+ (PRTG went from $4 to $20+, ONCO moved $1+)

R-MULTIPLE TARGETING:
- Risk/reward ratio must favor you
- Example: Risk $100 to make $300+ (3:1 ratio)
- 80% win rate not necessary - 60-70% win rate sufficient with good R:R
- Even perfect setups occasionally fail, so manage accordingly

MACD AS EXIT TRIGGER:
- When MACD crosses over (lines converge and flip) = exit signal
- BEST TRADES: On front side of move before MACD squeeze
- Once moving averages converge (consolidation phase) = choppy/risky
- Exit when consolidation begins, don't fight the chop

TIME-BASED EXITS:
- Don't hold winners into market close (3:55-4 PM)
- Take profits before close if still holding
- Overnight risk is unpredictable (overnight news, gaps)
- Day trader mentality: Close out positions, reset next day

SWING TRADE HOLDS (EXTENDED):
- If entry is very early with huge potential = can hold longer
- Monitor intra-day consolidation patterns
- Exit at next MACD squeeze or consolidation area
- Not typical for day trading - usually quick in/out

EXIT RULES SUMMARY:
1. First sign of reversal (two lower closes) = exit consideration
2. MACD crosses negative = exit
3. High volume selling after your entry = exit
4. Stop loss hit = exit immediately (no exceptions)
5. Take partial profits on 10-15% move
6. Trail stop on runner position
7. Close positions before market close
8. If stock is choppy = get out and move to next setup

6. RISK MANAGEMENT & POSITION SIZING
================================================================================

POSITION SIZING METHODOLOGY:

INITIAL STARTING POINT:
- Daily profit goal: $10-20 minimum (proof of concept level)
- Start with small consistent gains - scale ONLY after proving consistency
- Example: One student traded $10/day consistently, then scaled to $350k/year
- Proof of concept = ability to be profitable before increasing size

SHARE SIZE SCALING RULES:

RULE: Scale size ONLY after proving track record of consistency
- Never increase share size during a downward spiral
- Never increase share size while on emotional high from big win
- Size increase: 10-15% at a time, only after 4-6 weeks of consistent profits

POSITION SIZE GROWTH PATTERN:
- Positive feedback loop: Accuracy → Bigger wins → Confidence → Bigger position → Higher profits
- This grows accounts exponentially: Small, consistent profits compound
- Negative feedback loop: Loss → Emotional hijack → Bigger losses → Reduced confidence → Smaller account

ACCOUNT SIZE STRUCTURE (Ross's Model):

TRADING ACCOUNT:
- Keep only $150,000 maximum in active trading account
- This limits maximum daily loss possible
- Starting account: Can start with as little as $5,000
- As account grows, withdraw excess to separate retirement IRA

EXCESS PROFITS STRATEGY:
- When trading account reaches $250,000-$300,000
- Roll over $100,000 to long-term retirement account (IRA)
- Keeps trading account perpetually small and manageable
- Excess profits invest long-term (8% annual return target)

POSITION SIZING FORMULA:

BASED ON RISK TOLERANCE:
- Calculate daily loss comfort level (emotional limit)
- Work backward to determine position size
- Example: If loss comfort is $250, and stop loss is 2% away = position size of ~12,500 shares

TYPICAL POSITION RANGES:
- Small account: 100-1,000 shares per trade
- Medium account: 1,000-5,000 shares per trade
- Larger account: 5,000-15,000 shares per trade
- Ross's average: $8,000 per position (with $150k account)

NEVER:
- Risk more than 1-2% of your account per trade (standard risk management)
- Make a single position larger than you can emotionally handle a loss on
- Trade on margin until you're consistently profitable
- Add to losing positions (never "average down")

RISK PER TRADE CALCULATION:
1. Determine stop loss distance (cents away from entry)
2. Multiply by share size = total risk in dollars
3. Never exceed 1-2% of account balance
4. Example: 5,000 shares × $0.20 stop = $1,000 risk max (on $50k account = 2%)

LOSS COMFORT LEVEL SYSTEM:
- Define max loss you can emotionally handle per day
- Adjust position size to stay within that limit
- Small account traders: $50-200 daily loss comfort typical
- As account grows, dollar comfort grows but percentage stays 1-2%

7. MARKET CONDITIONS & TIMING
================================================================================

OPTIMAL MARKET CONDITIONS:

BEST TRADING SCENARIOS:
- Market in uptrend (major indices rising)
- Multiple stocks moving in same direction
- High market-wide volatility and volume
- Multiple news catalysts triggering moves

MARKET CONDITIONS TO AVOID:
- Market in downtrend or choppy consolidation
- Low overall market volume
- Conflicting news or uncertain economic events
- Pre-market/post-market extended hours (less liquid)

TIME OF DAY CONSIDERATIONS:

EARLY MORNING (7-10 AM):
- Many stocks moving before 9:30 AM open
- Early traders getting in on news they saw
- Most profitable time for some setups
- But: Simulator may not work early (think or swim only starts 9:30 AM)

MARKET OPEN WINDOW (9:30-10:15 AM):
- Peak volatility often occurs here
- Opening excitement and initial moves
- Good entry opportunities from overnight news
- Volume typically heaviest here

MID-MORNING (10:15 AM-12 PM):
- First consolidations forming
- Some momentum trades still available
- Less consistent than opening window

AFTERNOON (12 PM-3 PM):
- Generally slower, choppier action
- Fewer high-quality setups
- Risk of choppy, whipsaw trades
- Some day traders avoid this period

CLOSING HOUR (3-4 PM):
- Use for exits only, not entries
- Avoid holding positions overnight
- Close out all trades before 4 PM market close
- Overnight gaps and news unpredictable

EXTENDED HOURS (4 PM-8 PM):
- Not optimal for most day traders
- Lower liquidity = harder fills
- Less predictable action
- Riskier for small account traders

MARKET GAP CONSIDERATIONS:
- Check overnight news that could gap stocks
- If gap-up on catalyst: Can trade the first move
- If gap-down: Skip or size down
- Pre-market volume and sentiment important

8. TRADING PSYCHOLOGY / MENTAL RULES
================================================================================

PSYCHOLOGICAL FOUNDATION:

CRITICAL MINDSET: THE TWO-PATH CONCEPT

NEGATIVE SPIRAL PATH (What to Avoid):
1. Big unexpected loss
2. Secondary losses (losses building)
3. Average losers get BIGGER (trading from emotional compromise)
4. Total losses grow (account damage increases)
5. Loss of confidence and account reduction
6. Result: Account shrinks 50%+, requires 100%+ gain to recover to break-even

POSITIVE FEEDBACK LOOP PATH (What to Build):
1. Accuracy in trading (quality setups, good execution)
2. Strong profit-to-loss ratio (average winners bigger than average losers)
3. Confidence grows (proving you can do this)
4. Bigger share sizes taken (from confidence, not desperation)
5. Higher profits (from larger positions on winning setups)
6. More confidence (virtuous cycle)
7. Result: Exponential account growth

BEING A GOOD TRADER = BEING GOOD AT LOSING

Definition: The ability to:
- Recognize a losing trade quickly
- Cut losses without emotion
- Take the loss and move to next trade mentally
- Don't let one bad trade affect next decision
- Don't revenge-trade trying to recover losses

CRITICAL PSYCHOLOGICAL RULES:

RULE #1: FOLLOW THE RULES, ALWAYS
- You have the strategy, you know the rules
- Success depends 100% on discipline to follow them
- The ball is in your court - execute or fail
- No emotional exceptions to rules

RULE #2: MANAGE EMOTIONS THROUGH POSITION SIZING
- Keep share size well within loss comfort levels
- Emotional control = controlled position sizing
- Can't be calm while risking money you can't lose
- Small positions = clear thinking = better decisions

RULE #3: STRICT LOSS GUIDELINES
- Have written daily loss limits
- Have written stop loss rules
- Have written position size rules
- Refer to written rules, not emotions, when deciding

RULE #4: CONSISTENCY IS THE GOAL, NOT HUGE WINS
- Focus on consistent $10-50 daily profits
- Many small wins beat few huge wins with big losses
- Track metrics: Win rate, profit/loss ratio, daily results
- Trend matters more than individual trade

RULE #5: DON'T COMPARE TO OTHER TRADERS
- Every trader has different risk tolerance
- Someone risking $5k per trade has different curve
- Focus on YOUR consistency in YOUR strategy
- Green days for you matter, not green days for others

RULE #6: TAKE NOTES ON EMOTIONAL TRIGGERS
- What patterns cause you to break rules?
- Do certain stocks trigger impulse buys?
- Do certain losses cause revenge trading?
- Create a list and develop defensive strategies

TRIGGERS TO WATCH FOR:
- FOMO ("Fear of Missing Out") - stock moving and you jump in late
- Revenge trading - trying to immediately recover a loss
- Overconfidence - taking bigger size after big win
- Frustration - trading poor setups to "just make something"
- Chasing - entering on highs instead of dips
- Holding losers - "it has to bounce"
- Over-trading - making too many trades per day

HOW TO COMBAT EMOTIONAL TRADING:

1. MEDITATION / EXERCISE:
- Activities requiring discipline build mental strength
- Clear mind between trades = better decisions
- Journal your trades and results
- Review patterns (trades, setups, losses)

2. JOURNALING:
- Write down every trade: entry, exit, reason, profit/loss
- Review weekly: What worked? What didn't?
- Identify patterns in your losses
- Track which setups are most profitable for YOU

3. METRICS TRACKING:
- Track win rate % (goal: 60-70%)
- Track profit/loss ratio (average winner vs loser)
- Track daily profit/loss
- Track streaks (win streaks, loss streaks)
- Metrics give perspective and objectivity

4. BEHAVIORAL RULES:
- If you're emotional, reduce position size in half
- If you just took big loss, skip next trade (take a break)
- If account down 20%+ in a day, stop trading for the day
- If on loss streak, focus on quality only (skip questionable setups)

5. STOP TRADING IF:
- You feel angry or frustrated
- You're desperate to "make it back"
- You're trading to escape emotion rather than from plan
- You're questioning your strategy after one or two losses
- Your position size exceeds your loss comfort level

SELF-TALK AND MANTRAS:
- "Cut losses fast"
- "Consistency, not heroics"
- "Follow the rules"
- "One no = entire no"
- "If uncertain, pass"
- "Let winners run, cut losers quick"

- More predictable than initial break

THE POWER OF VOLUME PROFILE ANALYSIS:
- Volume bars tell story of supply vs demand
- Green volume = buying pressure (good for long)
- Red volume = selling pressure (bad for long)
- HIGH RED VOLUME = Major rejection, don't buy
- LIGHT RED VOLUME = Normal profit taking, acceptable
- Volume must support price action

CANDLESTICK REJECTION PATTERNS:
- "Topping tails" = upper wicks with small bodies
- Shows price rejected at higher level
- Indicates sellers taking control
- Don't buy on candles with topping tails
- Wait for reversal back up before entering


MARKET GAPS AND OPENING STRATEGY:

PRE-MARKET GAPS:
- Check overnight news before market open
- Massive gap up = already moved, be cautious
- Massive gap down = damaged, consider waiting
- Small gap up on catalyst = opportunity for entry
- Gap-up follow-through depends on volume and news strength

OPENING BELL STRATEGY:
- First 5 minutes after 9:30 AM often volatile
- Let market settle slightly (watch first 2-3 minutes)
- Enter on first confirmed breakout after open
- Initial open moves can be false (not enough volume yet)


EXTENDED HOURS TRADING CONSIDERATIONS:

PRE-MARKET (4-9:30 AM):
- Lower liquidity (harder to get filled at expected price)
- Fewer traders participating
- Can find early moves on news
- Be cautious with size - may not get filled

POST-MARKET (4-8 PM):
- Even lower liquidity
- Wider spreads (bid-ask difference)
- News often hits during this time
- Not optimal for day traders

RECOMMENDATION:
- Focus on main market hours (9:30 AM - 4 PM)
- Occasionally trade pre-market for specific catalysts
- Avoid post-market unless exceptional situation
